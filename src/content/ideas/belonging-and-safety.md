More recent literature on digital nomadism offers both a useful parallel and a genuine point of divergence. Doğan's (2023) concept of the ‘neonomad’ - whose sense of belonging is built from ‘memories, experiences, and ongoing spatial practices’ rather than a fixed location - closely mirrors this project's settling-in marker findings, including the observation that carried objects can construct belonging on the move. However, a 2025 study of digital nomad belonging (Communications in Humanities Research) found that belonging among digital nomads is increasingly ‘centered on self-worth, professional identity, and social recognition’ rather than relationships in the conventional sense. This project's findings diverge from that conclusion: belonging in this sample is described overwhelmingly in relational and emotional terms, with comparatively little reference to professional identity or status. This divergence may reflect a genuine difference between digital-nomad populations (typically defined by remote knowledge work) and this project's sample (drawn from a volunteer-based, communally-structured hub), and is worth treating as a substantive finding about this specific population rather than a contradiction to be smoothed over.
