Directly asked whether they find home or create it, most participants offered some version of ‘both’. Close reading shows this single word covering at least five distinct positions.

> *“It cannot be separated.” — Karolina*
>
> *“There's a part that you have to create and a part that you have to find.” — Pau, via an analogy to love*
>
> *“In Poland I'm creating, and in this travel I'm finding.” — Erika*
>
> *“I think I find it. Maybe 90% of finding and 10% of creating.” — Soph*

Michel's account is the most theoretically interesting. He describes himself as finding home through research and planning, yet his clearest examples of feeling at home occur specifically when he does not know his departure date.

> *“Because I've been planning so much that I know when I'm going to departure, I don't really make the place my home.” — Michel, whose stated theory and own behaviour disagree*

Bella offers the sample's most deliberately-worked version of ‘both’, articulated twice: once conversationally, and once in a song she co-wrote with university roommates, written on the day they moved out.

> *“Found it here, we'll find it anywhere... built it here, we'll build it anywhere.” — from a song Bella co-wrote with university roommates on the day they moved out*


Theo offers the clearest unambiguous exception - the only unqualified ‘create, not find’ position in the dataset, explicitly contrasting himself with people who report instant resonance with a place:

> *“I think it can't be found, I think you have to create it yourself... there's no perfect place for anyone where you just arrive and this is perfection.” — Theo*

Paulina and Emma each add further variants: Paulina describes creating as her default strategy but with an explicit exit clause (“When I don't find it or can't create it, I leave”); Emma locates creation specifically inside herself, as an internal psychological practice, rather than as something built externally in a place.
